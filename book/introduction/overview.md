# Overview of the tutorial

The tutorial is organized around one practical question: **which LLM
capabilities matter for music, and how can we deliberately use them?**

## Structure

### Part 0 · Introduction (5 min)

- Background: LLMs and Agents — from language models to foundation models, and why LLMs and agents matter for music
- Overview of Tutorial — structure and learning goals of the tutorial

### Part I · Foundations

- 1 · Overview of Large Language Models and Agents (30 min) — autoregressive modeling, pre-, mid-, and post-training, advanced capabilities, and evaluation
- 2 · LLM-powered Music Understanding (30 min) — music perception and reasoning, large music-language models across audio, score images, symbolic notation, and metadata, and benchmarks

### Part II · Applications

- 3 · Music Listening — music annotation and description, conversational retrieval and recommendation
- 4 · Composition & Co-Creation — human-AI collaborative songwriting, composition support tools, and conversational post-production
- 5 · Feedback & Music Education — multimodal understanding of performances, assessment and coaching, and interactive music education

### Wrap-up

- Conclusion — recap, future research directions, Q&A and discussion
- References — works cited across the tutorial

## Learning goals

By the end of the tutorial, participants should be able to:

1. connect autoregressive modeling and post-training to modern LLM behavior;
2. compare architectures for audio, score-image, symbolic, and text inputs;
3. select capabilities such as instruction following, reasoning, or tool use for a musical task;
4. identify suitable benchmarks and limitations for a music-language system.

## How to use this book

Each chapter has a web page, slides, and reproducible code on
[GitHub](https://github.com/music-llm-tutorial/music-llm-tutorial.github.io).
Chapters in Part II can be read in any order once Part I is familiar.
